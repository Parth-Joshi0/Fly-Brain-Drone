--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHERE THIS PROJECT WAS BEFORE THE SESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The repo runs a real slice of the fly connectome as a live controller for a
DJI Tello. One circuit existed:

    268 LC4 + LPLC2 neurons  (looming-sensitive visual projection neurons)
            |  7,973 real FlyWire v630 synapses
            v
    6 descending neurons: DNp01, DNp03, DNp06  (left and right of each)

DNp01 is the Giant Fiber, the fly's escape-command neuron. Optic-flow expansion
from the camera drives the LC4/LPLC2 cells via Poisson input, the network runs
in Brian2 for 20 ms per control tick, and the DNs' firing becomes an escape
dodge. That circuit was already validated on real hardware (5/5 swat detections,
0 false positives, desk test, 2026-09-25).

The weakness: every output is ONE neuron. A single real neuron fires 0-4 times
per 20 ms window, which is a jumpy 0/50/100/150 Hz staircase, so the readout had
to be heavily EMA-smoothed to be flyable. RATE_SMOOTHING = 0.25 exists purely to
hide that.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHY DNg02, AND WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DNg02 is a POPULATION - at least 15 near-identical cell pairs. Namiki et al.
2022 (Current Biology 32(5):1189-1196) showed that optogenetically recruiting
more DNg02 pairs raises wingbeat amplitude roughly linearly, ~2.77 degrees per
pair. The fly encodes flight power in HOW MANY of them are firing.

So the readout is a recruitment COUNT, not a rate. No smoothing trick needed to
make it continuous - it is continuous because there are 24 cells. That is the
whole reason this was the right circuit to build next.

The paper also found:
  - Left and right DNg02 act independently.
  - Symmetric activity  -> bilateral wingbeat change -> THRUST.
  - Opponent activity    -> asymmetric wingbeat      -> YAW (steering).
  - "Rightward motion elicited an increase in activity of the right DNg02 cells
    and a simultaneous DECREASE in the left."  The decrease matters - see 4.3.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT THE CONNECTOME ACTUALLY SAID (all verified against this repo's parquet)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1  25 DNg02 neurons exist in FlyWire v630, subtypes DNg02_a..DNg02_h,
     13 left / 12 right, mostly cholinergic. 24 are in this repo's own
     completeness list; the 25th (a right cell) is dropped, so the modelled
     population is 13 left / 11 right.

3.2  THE POPULATION CODE IS REAL IN THE DATA. Excitatory input weight per
     DNg02 cell, sorted, spans ~700x in a smooth monotone ladder:

       727 684 632 602 590 560 351 310 272 251 179 130 117 101 95 95 44 31 ...

     Under a common drive, cells recruit roughly in that order, so the count is
     a monotone analog signal. Left and right are interleaved and near-balanced.
     This gradient IS the population code, and it was already sitting in the
     connectome data the repo had.

3.3  DNg02 HAS NO USABLE VISUAL INPUT IN THIS CONNECTOME. Of 12,848 incoming
     synapses: 137 (1%) from visual projection neurons, ZERO from the HS/VS/H2
     wide-field tangential cells that carry optic flow, and ZERO from the
     LC4/LPLC2 pool above (the 7 synapses from the looming circuit are
     DNp01/03/06 -> DNg02, not LC4/LPLC2 -> DNg02).

     Its input is central: posterior slope (PS005/008/109/180/181/182),
     inferior bridge (IB008/010/025), clamp (CL216/336/171/155), LAL197/200,
     and ascending neurons from the ventral nerve cord.

     This is NOT a modelling failure. It independently reproduces a published
     open question - Schnell 2026, Trends in Neurosciences 49:98-110, states
     that DNg02's "connectivity with LPTCs or other visually responsive neurons
     remains to be established."

     *** THE ONE MODELLING ASSUMPTION IN THIS CIRCUIT ***
     Optic flow is injected into those central cells. The connectome does not
     say they carry a motion signal. Everything DOWNSTREAM of that injection is
     measured, not assumed: which cell excites or inhibits which DNg02, how
     strongly, and therefore the recruitment order. The recruitment order is
     the scientifically interesting result. This is not "we found DNg02's
     visual pathway." Say it that way in any write-up.

3.4  The drive pool is only weakly lateralised overall (ipsi/contra ~1.1), so a
     naive hemifield split produces a fixed bias, not a flow-dependent
     differential. Lateralised SUBSETS do exist, and the largest single one is
     ipsilateral INHIBITION (211 cells, weight 1,710). That matters because the
     paper's own result - the left cells DECREASING - cannot come from
     excitation at all.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  build_dng02_circuit.py                                            (NEW)

     Reproducible builder for dng02_circuit_neurons.json. Downloads the FlyWire
     annotation table (cached, gitignored, 31 MB), cross-references this repo's
     completeness list and connectivity parquet, and writes the 24 DNg02 cells
     plus a 120-neuron drive pool. Every selection rule is a constant at the top
     of the file, and running it prints the resulting circuit so the output is
     self-checking.

     Why it exists: looming_circuit_neurons.json was produced by hand with no
     build script, so nobody could re-derive it or change a threshold and see
     the effect. This fixes that pattern going forward.

     Per driver it records, measured from the parquet: sign (+1 excitatory /
     -1 inhibitory), w_left and w_right (how its influence splits across the two
     halves of the population), and ipsi_frac.

         conda run -n brian2 python build_dng02_circuit.py

4.2  fly_brain_controller.py                                      (EXTENDED)

     One merged Brian2 network: 418 neurons, 10,911 synapses, ~18 ms per 20 ms
     step against a 33 ms budget.

     WHY ONE NETWORK, MEASURED NOT ASSUMED: Brian2's cost is per-OBJECT, not
     per-neuron. Adding DNg02 as a second PoissonGroup + Synapses pair measured
     20.33 ms/step; folding it into the existing PoissonGroup as extra indices
     measured 17.40 ms. Same neurons, same synapses, 2.9 ms difference. Hence
     one resized group and explicit index arrays instead of connect(j="i").

     GATED AT BUILD TIME: with_dng02=False builds literally the original
     274-neuron network, same object graph, same Poisson RNG stream. Verified
     bit-identical against the git HEAD version across 80 windows. So leaving
     optomotor off does not merely leave escape behaviour statistically
     similar - it leaves it identical. That was a hard requirement because the
     escape flight test was being tuned in parallel.

     THE DRIVE RULE. Each driver's Poisson rate is:

         req_i  = frac_left_i * req_left + frac_right_i * req_right
         rate_i = clip(REST + GAIN * sign_i * req_i, 0, 1) * MAX_DRIVE_RATE

     The sign_i factor is essential: 73 of the 120 drivers are INHIBITORY, so
     "recruit more DNg02" has to be expressible as those cells firing LESS.
     Without it, raising the common drive would SUPPRESS the population. It is
     also what reproduces the paper's opponent decrease. DRIVE_REST_FRACTION
     exists so inhibitory cells have something to decrease from; a request of
     exactly zero on every channel bypasses it and holds the pool silent.

     THE READOUT (_read_dng02):
         n_left, n_right  = how many DNg02 spiked this 20 ms window
         thrust = (smoothed n_left + n_right) / DNG02_RECRUIT_SAT
         steer  = act_right - act_left, via per-side curve inversion

     Two subtleties, both measured:
       - thrust is NOT divided by 24. The population never all fires; 13.1 of
         24 at maximum drive, and the bottom third of the ladder never recruits
         at all. Dividing by 24 would cap thrust at ~0.54 and waste half the
         range. DNG02_RECRUIT_SAT = 13.1 is measured.
       - steer uses per-side CURVE INVERSION, not scaling. The right-side cells
         receive far more input than the left (1,857 vs 1,119 total excitatory
         weight), and the two sides' recruitment curves differ in SHAPE, not
         just gain. A single scale factor leaves a standing bias at intermediate
         drive, which on a drone reads as a constant slow yaw. Each side's count
         is mapped back through its own measured curve onto a common axis
         before differencing, so symmetric drive gives zero at every level.

     The ready handshake now reports the network's own constants and the DNg02
     labels in ladder order, because the parent process cannot import this
     module (no brian2 there, deliberately) and a log should record what the
     circuit was ACTUALLY built with rather than what the caller assumed.

4.3  neural_pathways/escape_neuron/optical_flow.py                                       (EXTENDED)

     signed_hemifield_flow(flow, ...) -> {left, right, rotation, translation}

     grid_flow_strengths returns MAGNITUDES, which cannot express direction, so
     it cannot drive steering at all. And a per-hemifield value alone cannot
     tell rotation from approach. They separate cleanly by symmetry:

         rotating   -> scene slides the same way in BOTH hemifields
         moving fwd -> scene slides OUTWARD, opposite signs

     so  rotation    = (left + right) / 2   isolates rotation
         translation = (right - left) / 2   isolates forward motion

     Two means over an array the caller already has. Without that decomposition
     a drone reads the outward flow of an approaching wall as a turn and yaws at
     everything it flies toward.

     derotate_flow gained a pixels_per_radian keyword (default unchanged, so
     every existing caller behaves exactly as before). See section 6.2.

4.4  neural_pathways/flybrain_controller.py                           (EXTENDED)

     New keyword-only argument: optomotor=False. Off by default, and when off
     the brain subprocess is not even told to build the DNg02 half.

     When on, _optomotor_drive turns flow into a drive request:
       - STEERING is opponent, magnitude from uncommanded rotation, direction
         from its sign. rotation > 0 means the scene slides right, i.e. the
         drone is rotating LEFT without being asked, so the correction is to
         yaw right, which means favouring the RIGHT DNg02.
       - THRUST is SET-POINT regulation, not proportional to flow. This is a
         stability requirement, not a style choice: Namiki measured DNg02 open
         loop, but in closed loop the drone's own speed generates the flow, so
         "more flow -> more thrust" is positive feedback and it accelerates
         until something stops it. Flies hold a preferred image velocity, which
         is negative feedback. So thrust rises when flow is BELOW the setpoint.
       - STEERING GETS HEADROOM: drive_common is capped at 1 - |offset|. The
         brain clips each side to +-1, so without the cap a common drive near
         1.0 pins BOTH sides at the ceiling and the opponent channel goes dead.
         That happened on 18% of cycles in the first real run. It is not a
         desk-only problem: hover, slow flight, climbing and any low-texture
         scene sit below the flow setpoint, so steering authority would collapse
         exactly when course-holding matters most. Thrust yields because
         recruitment is already near saturation up there.

     *** THE SIGN, AND IT IS THE OPPOSITE OF DNp06's ***
     DNg02 activity correlates with wingbeat amplitude in the CONTRALATERAL
     wing. More right-side DNg02 means a bigger LEFT wingbeat, which yaws the
     fly RIGHT. This project's convention is positive yaw_rate = turn LEFT.
     Therefore:
             yaw_rate -= DNG02_YAW_GAIN * steer
     DNp06 is ADDED two lines away, because that circuit steers AWAY from a
     looming object, which is already positive-is-left. Two opposite conventions
     in adjacent lines, both deliberate, both commented. Do not "fix" either.

     ESCAPE STAYS STRICTLY DOMINANT, two independent ways: the ESCAPE branch of
     decide() is untouched by the optomotor path, AND every DNg02 term is scaled
     by (1 - escape).

4.5  Drone/tello_drone.py                                    (CHANGED)

     TELLO_YAW_SIGN = -1.0, applied once where yaw enters get_state().

     The Tello reports yaw increasing CLOCKWISE (turning right); this project's
     convention is positive = counter-clockwise (turning left). Nothing flipped
     it. Converting at the boundary means yaw_rate, the orientation quaternion,
     yaw_degrees and the dead-reckoned position are all in one convention.
     A named constant rather than an inline negation because it is a MEASURED
     value that a firmware update could invalidate - and the calibration mode
     now re-checks it (section 5.1).

     Note the dead-reckoned position was also integrating a clockwise heading
     through a formula expecting counter-clockwise, so its path was mirrored.

     tello_neuron_test.py and tello_dng02_test.py read attitude themselves
     rather than through get_state, so they import and apply the same constant.
     Change one and you must change the others.

5. THE TESTS, AND WHAT EACH IS FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1  neural_pathways/tests/test_dng02_circuit.py            brian2 env, no drone, ~2 min

     The characterisation gate. Nothing DNg02-related should reach a drone until
     this passes. Prints the recruitment ladder, every synapse from the drive
     pool back into the escape circuit split by sign, a symmetric drive sweep, an
     asymmetric sweep, and a 2-D loom x drive sweep. 11 hard assertions.

     The load-bearing ones: step time p95 under 33 ms; population silent when
     idle; recruitment monotone AND graded (a population that switches on all
     at once has no code left to read); steer correctly signed both ways with no
     standing bias at symmetric drive; DNp01 NEVER spiking from optic-flow drive
     alone; and the ESCAPE threshold not moving between zero and full drive.

     It also prints DNG02_RECRUIT_SAT and the two recruitment curves as
     paste-ready lines, so those calibration constants stay regenerable instead
     of becoming magic numbers.

         conda run -n brian2 python neural_pathways/tests/test_dng02_circuit.py

5.2  neural_pathways/tests/test_optomotor_sign.py           normal env, no drone, ~1 min

     Locks the sign of the closed loop. Drives the whole adapter chain with
     synthetic flow and asserts on the COMMANDED YAW, which is what reaches the
     motors. Run it after touching anything in the optomotor path.

     It exists because a flipped sign does not make the drone fly slightly
     worse - it yaws harder into its own drift until it hits something, and
     test_dng02_circuit.py cannot catch it because it never sees a yaw command.
     13 checks, including escape dominance and that optomotor=False is inert.

5.3  Drone/tests/test_tello_harness_smoke.py      normal env, no drone, ~1 min

     Runs the real main() of both Tello scripts against a FAKE drone - fake
     attitude, fake moving frames, cv2's display calls stubbed so the HUD code
     still executes without a window. Every mode x the video path.

     RUN THIS BEFORE EVERY HARDWARE SESSION. It exists because the per-cycle
     body and the cv2 HUD were the only code in this project whose first
     execution was always on a real drone, mid-session, with a battery burning.
     A stale dict key in one HUD f-string - invisible to import, to --analyze
     and to --no-video - crashed a calibration run one cycle after warmup and
     cost a whole session. This catches that class of bug in under a minute.

5.4  Drone/tests/tello_dng02_test.py              REAL Tello, PROPS OFF, no flight

     Never calls takeoff(), land() or send_rc_control(). Imports the harness
     helpers from tello_neuron_test.py rather than copying them, so the
     placeholder-frame guard, duplicate-frame rejection and attitude handling
     stay in one place.

     --mode calibrate   Rotate the drone by hand. Regresses raw horizontal flow
                        against the Tello's own attitude to re-check
                        TELLO_YAW_SIGN and estimate pixels_per_radian.
                        THE FIT IS WINDOWED, and that is not a refinement: the
                        Tello streams attitude at ~9 Hz while frames arrive at
                        20-25 Hz, so 56-70% of frames report a yaw change of
                        exactly zero and the rest report a jump that did not
                        happen over the interval the flow was measured across.
                        Same data: R^2 0.38 per-frame vs 0.89 over 20 frames.
                        Windowing also absorbs the 1-degree yaw quantisation.
                        The TREND across window sizes is itself the diagnosis.

     --mode pan         Sweep a textured target. The population-code check. HUD
                        draws the population as two rows of cells in ladder
                        order so you can watch them recruit in sequence.
                        d / a latch a sweep direction, x clears.

     --analyze PATH     Scores a log. No drone, no brain. 8-9 PASS/FAIL checks.

     Why --analyze exists: the escape circuit could be scored by counting
     discrete events against SPACE markers. DNg02 has no events, only the shape
     of a distribution, and the HUD updates 25 times a second while the eye
     averages it. "The cells lit up in roughly the right order" is not something
     you can honestly judge by watching.

     It reports: a per-cell recruitment table in ladder order; Spearman rho
     between ladder position and firing rate (wants NEGATIVE); gradedness;
     the steering channel binned by |rotation|; the thrust channel binned by
     drive_common; baseline noise vs the steering floor; the sign recomputed
     from the rows; and timing.

     It averages MAGNITUDES, not signed values - a bin mixes leftward and
     rightward sweeps, so a signed mean of steer averages +0.4 and -0.4 to
     nearly nothing however strong the response was.

6. WHAT THE HARDWARE RUNS ACTUALLY MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1  THE POPULATION CODE WORKS ON REAL TELLO VIDEO.  *** MAIN RESULT ***

     Two independent props-off runs:

         run 1 (dng02_pan.log)   rho = -0.850,  15 of 24 cells recruited
         run 2 (dng02_pan2.log)  rho = -0.876,  15 of 24 cells recruited
         onset rho = +0.818 in both

     And robust across operating regimes, checked on run 1:

         all live cycles                     n=1002   rho -0.850
         saturated (drive_common >= 0.99)    n= 743   rho -0.804
         unsaturated                         n= 259   rho -0.832
         mid-range recruitment (4-11 cells)  n= 156   rho -0.842

     Cells recruit in the order the connectome's synaptic weights predict, on
     real H.264 video from a real camera. That is the DNg02 claim, measured.

     Timing: brain 18.9 ms mean, full cycle 31.7 mean / 33.3 p95. Comfortably
     inside budget, and slightly better than the pre-DNg02 escape runs.

6.2  TELLO_YAW_SIGN CONFIRMED.  *** SECOND RESULT, AND IT REACHES THE ESCAPE PATH ***

     Before the fix (dng02_calibrate.log):  slope -86.6, R^2 0.888, negative at
                                            every window size from 1 to 30.
     After  the fix (dng02_calibrate3.log): slope POSITIVE at every window size
                                            (45 -> 208), R^2 up to 0.94.

     So the Tello's yaw genuinely runs the opposite way, and the conversion is
     now correct. Before this, derotate_flow ADDED the drone's own rotation
     instead of cancelling it, and LoomingDetector's rotation-removal homography
     warped the wrong way and ADDED apparent expansion during every turn. That
     affected the ESCAPE path, not just DNg02.

     THE MAGNITUDE, HOWEVER, CANNOT BE MEASURED THIS WAY. Binning by rotation
     size on the good run shows the flow-to-rotation ratio FALLING:

         rotation ~1.00 deg/frame -> 3.21 px  ->  184 px/rad
         rotation ~1.21 deg/frame -> 3.64 px  ->  172 px/rad
         rotation ~2.00 deg/frame -> 3.70 px  ->  106 px/rad
         rotation ~3.75 deg/frame -> 3.88 px  ->   59 px/rad

     compute_flow's Farneback settings (levels=2, winsize=13) under-track the
     displacement from about 1 deg/frame upward - far earlier than expected. So
     there is no rotation speed that is both above the 1-degree telemetry
     quantisation and below Farneback's limit. Rotating slower does not fix it.

     BUT THE ANSWER IS ALREADY IN THE CODEBASE. The correct near-axis px/rad is
     the camera's focal length, and LoomingDetector already computes it:

         f = (height/2) / tan(fov/2) = 120 / tan(27.8 deg) = 227.6 px/rad

     The measurement brackets it - through-origin fits at the best-R^2 windows
     gave 243 (w40), 252 (w50), 274 (w60), 280 (w80) - and the free and
     through-origin fits agree to within 0.5%, so the line does pass through the
     origin as physics requires.

     RECOMMENDATION: _PIXELS_PER_RADIAN should be ~228 for the Tello, not 75.
     I did NOT change it, because it is the default for derotate_flow and
     therefore feeds the ESCAPE path's magnitude channel, whose thresholds are
     hand-tuned and being tuned by someone else right now. See section 7.

6.3  THE STEERING CHANNEL IS STILL NOT PROPERLY EXERCISED, AND THE PAN
     PROTOCOL IS WHY.

         run 2, binned by |rotation|:
             4 of 5 bins had |rotation| between 0.00 and 0.04
             top bin only 0.43
             mean|steer| 0.056 -> 0.106

     Sweeping a card in front of a STATIONARY camera barely produces
     common-mode flow, because the background does not move - the hemifield
     average is diluted by the static scene. The optomotor channel responds to
     the WHOLE SCENE moving.

     Evidence it does work when properly driven: the calibrate run, where the
     drone itself was rotated, logged 86 cycles in the OPTOMOTOR state.

     FIX THE PROTOCOL, NOT THE CODE: to test steering props-off, ROTATE THE
     DRONE (as in calibrate mode) rather than moving a card, or fill the entire
     field of view with the moving target.

     Also on a desk drive_common stays pinned near 1.0, because translational
     flow is ~0.01 against a setpoint of 1.2. The headroom cap means the
     opponent channel still works, but recruitment sits at the top of its range
     (deciles 11-14), so gradedness cannot be fully demonstrated without flight.

6.4  THE BASELINE FLOOR NEEDS RAISING SLIGHTLY.

         run 2 quiet |rotation|: mean 0.023, p95 0.085, max 0.150
         OPTOMOTOR_FLOW_FLOOR  = 0.15

     Right at the edge - it fails by 0.000. Raise it to about 0.25 before any
     optomotor flight, or the drone will steer at its own Farneback noise.
     (Run 1's apparent failure at max 2.225 was a contaminated baseline -
     something crossed the lens 5 s in, brightness swung 126 -> 72 -> 156. The
     genuinely still window of run 1 had max 0.081.)

6.5  THE ROTATION-VS-ESCAPE TEST IS INCONCLUSIVE.  (neuron_yawfix.log)

         baseline_expansion  mean 0.751, p95 3.003, max 5.254
         vs LOOM_EXPANSION_FLOOR = 0.8
         5 escape triggers, 150 ESCAPE cycles

     Compare with the stationary desk run of 2026-09-25: mean 0.114, p95 0.159,
     max 0.195. Twenty times quieter.

     The difference is that the drone was HAND-HELD this time. Hand-held
     rotation inevitably translates and shakes, and translation genuinely
     produces expansion. So this run cannot separate "rotation still trips
     ESCAPE" from "my hands moved it". It does not show the yaw fix failed; it
     shows the experiment could not isolate the variable.

     TO DO IT PROPERLY: put the drone on something that rotates about its own
     axis without translating - a lazy susan, a turntable, a swivel chair seat -
     and repeat. That isolates rotation from translation.

7. STATUS: WHAT IS SETTLED, WHAT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONFIRMED
  - The population code recruits in connectome-predicted order on real video.
    Two runs, rho -0.85 and -0.88. This is the result.
  - TELLO_YAW_SIGN = -1.0 is correct. Measured both before and after.
  - Escape behaviour is bit-identical with optomotor=False.
  - No excitatory path from the drive pool to DNp01, so optic flow provably
    cannot trip the Giant Fiber. Asserted against live spikes.
  - Step time fits the 30 Hz budget with room to spare.

NOT SETTLED - DO NOT FLY THE OPTOMOTOR LOOP UNTIL THESE ARE DONE
  *** SUPERSEDED 2026-09-30 - see section 11. Items 1-3 were resolved or
  re-assessed and the yaw-only loop has flown. Kept as written for the record. ***
  1. _PIXELS_PER_RADIAN is still 75.0 (sim-derived). Should be ~228. Changing
     it also changes the escape path's magnitude channel, so coordinate before
     touching it. Until it is right, the optomotor efference copy leaves most of
     the drone's own commanded yaw in the signal, WITH THE SAME SIGN AS A REAL
     DRIFT - that is positive feedback.
  2. OPTOMOTOR_FLOW_FLOOR should go from 0.15 to ~0.25.
  3. The steering channel has never been driven hard, because the pan protocol
     does not produce common-mode flow. Re-test by rotating the drone.
  4. Pitch and roll signs are still unverified. They feed the same quaternion
     and the same homography as yaw, and sit near zero on a desk, so no
     props-off test can measure them. Only flight will.
  5. Thrust is untestable on a desk - a card sweep makes no translational flow.
  6. No flight test has been written for the optomotor loop, deliberately. Its
     prerequisites (items 1-3) are not met, and item 1 is a no-fly condition.

ALSO OUTSTANDING
  - The escape flight test needs re-validating. Its perception is now different
    during any turn because of the yaw fix, so previously tuned thresholds will
    have shifted. That file was deliberately never modified in this session.
  - brian2 falls back to numpy codegen on this machine (Cython test-compile
    fails). Still within budget at ~18 ms/step. `conda install -n brian2 cython`
    would be free headroom nobody has taken.

pins which interpreter hosts it; that matters because two brian2 installs were
once measured at 20 ms/step and 47 ms/step, i.e. the difference between meeting
and missing the budget.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. THE SHORT VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Built a second real connectome circuit - 24 DNg02 flight-motor descending
neurons plus their 120-neuron drive pool - alongside the existing looming/escape
circuit, in one Brian2 network, gated so escape behaviour is bit-identical when
the new path is off.

The point of DNg02 is that it is a POPULATION, so its output is a recruitment
count rather than one neuron's firing rate. Measured on real Tello video, the
cells recruit in the order the connectome's synaptic weights predict
(Spearman rho -0.85 and -0.88 across two runs). That is a real population code
running on real hardware.

Along the way the calibration work found that the Tello's reported yaw runs
opposite to this project's convention, which had been silently corrupting the
rotation-removal in the ESCAPE path too. That is fixed and confirmed.

The optomotor flight loop is NOT ready to fly: _PIXELS_PER_RADIAN is still
wrong, the steering channel has never been properly driven, and pitch/roll
signs are unverified. Sections 6 and 7 say exactly what to do about each.

11. 2026-09-30 - FIRST OPTOMOTOR FLIGHTS (yaw only)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11.1  _PIXELS_PER_RADIAN = 75 IS NOT A NO-FLY CONDITION. Section 7 item 1 had
      the direction of the risk backwards. derotate_flow subtracts the MEASURED
      yaw rate, and Farneback's measured flow-per-radian at flight yaw rates is
      106-184 (section 6.2). 75 is below all of it, so the drone's own rotation
      is always UNDER-cancelled, the residual keeps the sign of the real
      rotation, and the optomotor loop opposes it - a damper. OVER-cancelling
      (e.g. the geometric 228) flips the residual and is the positive-feedback
      case. So 75 stays, which also leaves the escape path untouched.

11.2  OPTOMOTOR_FLOW_FLOOR raised 0.15 -> 0.25 (section 6.4).
      test_optomotor_sign.py still passes.

11.3  Drone/tests/tello_optomotor_flight_test.py (NEW). Hover; the ONLY brain
      output flown is DNg02 yaw. Forward/strafe zero, DNp06 yaw dropped,
      ESCAPE -> hover, so exactly one loop is closed through the airframe.
      Yaw rate is differenced over 0.25 s (attitude is ~9 Hz vs 20-25 Hz
      video). Auto-land on |yaw| > 1.2 rad/s for 0.5 s. --dry-run never takes
      off or sends RC (the smoke test runs it against a FakeTello that raises
      on takeoff/land/RC). --yaw-gain overrides DNG02_YAW_GAIN, because at
      0.15 the desk-measured |steer| ~0.1 is ~1% yaw RC - below what the Tello
      visibly responds to.
