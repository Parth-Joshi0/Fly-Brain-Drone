Part 01 · Perception

Teaching a drone to find (and judge) bananas

Fruit flies are drawn to ripening fruit. To give FuityFly a "hunger" drive, we built a two-stage vision model that finds bananas in the Tello's video and grades their ripeness into six classes.

Akshin Makkar, Parth Joshi, Amal Chalayil Sreekumar food_detection/

1.1Why two stages?

"Where is the banana?" and "how ripe is it?" are different problems. Localisation is solved well by off-the-shelf detectors: COCO already has a banana class. Ripeness is fine-grained colour and texture judgement that COCO knows nothing about.

So we split the task. A pretrained YOLOv8n detector[1] proposes banana boxes, and a custom MobileNetV2 classifier[2] labels each crop. Both are small enough to run on a laptop receiving the Tello's video stream in real time.

Class imbalance. The largest class, rotten, has about 10× more training images than the smallest, unripe (1,340 vs 133). Plain accuracy can hide poor performance on rare classes, so per-class metrics matter here.

1.4Model: transfer learning on MobileNetV2

MobileNetV2 is built from inverted residual blocks with depthwise-separable convolutions. That gives strong ImageNet features at a small fraction of the compute of larger CNNs, which matters when the classifier runs once per detected banana in every frame.

We freeze the entire feature extractor and replace only the classifier head:

food_detection/model.py
def build_model(num_classes):
    model = models.mobilenet_v2(weights='IMAGENET1K_V1')

    for param in model.features.parameters():
        param.requires_grad = False

    model.classifier = nn.Sequential(
        nn.Dropout(0.2),
        nn.Linear(model.last_channel, num_classes)
    )
    return model

Only the final linear layer is trained, mapping the 1,280-dimensional pooled feature \(\mathbf{h}\) to six logits:

$$p(c \mid \mathbf{x}) = \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(W\,\operatorname{dropout}(\mathbf{h}) + \mathbf{b}\big)_c, \qquad W \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times 1280}$$(1)

That is 7,686 trainable parameters (6 × 1,280 weights + 6 biases). With about 4k training images, a small head like this is much less likely to overfit than fine-tuning the whole network.

Augmentation (train)Random horizontal flip, ±15° rotation, colour jitter (brightness/contrast/saturation 0.2)

Design decision · gentle colour jitter. Colour is the main cue for ripeness, since green, yellow and brown are what separate the classes. Jitter is kept mild (±20%): enough to make the model robust to camera white balance, but not so strong that it blurs the colour boundaries between classes.

1.6Confidence thresholds

On a flying drone, a missed banana just means searching a little longer. A false banana makes the drone "feed" on nothing. We tuned thresholds separately for the library default and for flight:

StageLibrary defaultIn flight
YOLOv8n detection confidence0.150.20
Ripeness classifier confidence0.000.10

Sources: BananaModel/banana_detector.py (BananaDetector defaults) and NeuralPathways/FoodNeuron/feeding_behaviour.py.

The detector threshold is deliberately low. COCO's banana class is a general-purpose category, and a drone's-eye view of a single banana, often green or brown and at a distance, tends to score low even when the detection is correct.

Observed in flight

Across the banana-seeking flights logged on the physical Tello (flight_logs/, five sessions), detector confidence on real bananas ranged from about 0.23 to 0.75, and classifier confidence from about 0.55 to 0.85. The ripeness label sometimes flipped between frames on the same banana (for example rotten ↔ freshripe). This is expected: motion blur, lighting and the drone's changing viewpoint all move the crop.

These figures are illustrative ranges from flight logs, not a controlled benchmark.

1.8Limitations & next steps

  • Temporal smoothing: a majority vote over the last few frames would remove the label flicker seen in flight.
  • Imbalance: class-weighted loss or oversampling unripe should help the rarest class.
  • Domain gap: the training images are not from the Tello's camera. A small set of drone-captured crops for fine-tuning would close the gap.
  • Partial fine-tuning: unfreezing the last few inverted-residual blocks is a natural next experiment once a baseline accuracy is recorded.
